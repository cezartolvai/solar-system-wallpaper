Solar System — information

Everything on this page is drawn by hand with Canvas 2D: no WebGL, no libraries, no textures, no network access. The camera transform, perspective projection, depth sorting, Kepler orbits and the lighting model are all implemented in the script below.

What is scientifically meaningful here

Positions come from real Keplerian orbital elements (J2000 epoch, with the standard per-century drift terms): semi-major axis, eccentricity, inclination, longitude of perihelion and of the ascending node for all eight planets, plus Mercury→Neptune mean-longitude rates. Each body is propagated with its own mean motion, so the planets spread out and re-align over decades exactly as their true periods dictate. The date shown is the real calendar date corresponding to the simulated instant.

  • real orbital elements, eccentricities, inclinations, nodal/perihelion geometry
  • real orbital periods, axial tilts, rotation periods (Venus and Uranus are retrograde)
  • real illumination geometry: the terminator is computed from the true Sun–body–camera vector, so moon phases and planet phases are correct
  • real Saturn's ring geometry (C / B / A rings with the Cassini division) and the planet's shadow falling across them

What is deliberately exaggerated or approximate

True scale is impossible on a screen: the Sun is 109 Earth radii wide but only 0.0046 AU across, while Neptune orbits 30 AU out. Two independent non-linear display maps are therefore used:

Distancesd_display = 24 · (r_AU)^0.62 — a power-law compression. Neptune sits at 198 units instead of 78× Mercury's 11.5–15 units, so both fit on screen together.
Body radiir_display = 1.5 · (r_km / 6371)^0.45 — radii are exaggerated by roughly 700×–2500× depending on the body (Jupiter is the mildest at ≈740×, Neptune the worst at ≈2540×; the Moon comes out at ≈310× because its orbit is inflated too). The exact factor for the selected body is printed in its fact sheet.
The SunCapped at 3.6 units so that it does not swallow Mercury's orbit (true proportion in this scene would be ≈12).
The MoonIts 384,400 km orbit is drawn at 3.0 units. This scene's own distance law would place it at 0.59 units (≈5× closer) and a strictly linear AU scale at 0.062 units (≈49× closer); either way it would disappear inside the drawn Earth, so the separation is inflated too.
RingsCorrect radii ratios, but flat (zero thickness) and semi-transparent.
Asteroids≈900 rocks on Keplerian orbits (real differential rotation by semi-major axis, Kirkwood-style density gaps at 2.5 / 2.82 / 2.95 AU) drawn far larger than reality.
Auxiliary motionsAxial precession, tides, relativity, planetary perturbations, moons other than ours are not modelled. The Moon uses a simplified two-body Kepler model (good to a few degrees). Surface markings are schematic, not maps. Longitudes are referred to the J2000 ecliptic, so equinox dates come out ~0.3 days late by 2024 (precession ≈ 50″/year is not applied).

So: use this page to build intuition about orbital geometry, period ratios and illumination — not as an ephemeris. approximate decorations are marked as such.

Rendering notes

  • Spheres are shaded as Lambertian discs: brightness ∝ max(0, n·L) with L = the true Sun direction, plus limb darkening and a soft terminator whose projected shape is the exact projection of the terminator great circle.
  • Depth uses painter's algorithm over a reused command pool: bodies, Saturn's 216 ring segments (3 zones × 72, each classified far/near of the planet) and every asteroid are sorted back-to-front each frame.
  • Backing-store resolution is capped and scaled adaptively if frame times rise, so high-DPI phones stay responsive.
